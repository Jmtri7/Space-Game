Default story — culture design atlas

Grown & Bolted

The default story names two peoples in a single sentence each: the Vherathi Concord grow their ships and cities from resin-crystal; the Drossholt Company bolt theirs together from salvage. This atlas draws that contrast all the way out — refined takes on the hulls, stations, outfits, buildings and props that exist today, plus new ships to fill the gaps, all held to a written silhouette rubric per culture.

This atlas shipped. Every ship, station, building and outfit plate below is now in config/stories/default, both station interiors and both moon cities are built to the floor-plan model, and the detailed silhouettes were extracted from these very SVGs into each entry's parts list — multi-polygon hulls with the plate's own edge colours. story.json is at 1.10.0; a save from before warns on load. See What shipped for the file-by-file map. Every ship plate (01·A / 02·A) also carries a drawn thruster port at each mount, extracted into parts; the exhaust flame stays procedural (Ship._draw_thrusters), drawn under the port and starting at the nozzle mouth.
Left — specimen
Top-down for ships & stations, front for figures, elevation for buildings & props. Framed against void-black at a fixed grid so silhouettes compare directly.
Shipped badge
Marks a plate that added a new key to config/stories/default and is now live. Plates with no badge refine an entry that already existed — those shipped too (see What shipped); the whole atlas is in game.
Spec block
Suggested values only. Ship stats mirror the three presets already tuned (shuttle / freighter / patrol) so autopilot behaviour stays predictable.
Rules row
Which of the culture's five silhouette directives the design leans on. The rubric is the point; the drawings are just it, applied.

Chapter 01 — The Vherathi Concord

Ships and cities grown, not assembled

Ships and cities grown from living resin-crystal rather than assembled from plate metal — architecture shaped the way coral shapes a reef: asymmetric, load-bearing ridges tapering toward one dominant point, with structural stress traced by thin veins of bioluminescent glass instead of punched-out windows. — cultures.json · "vherathi"
hull#4A3060 · metal_color
resin lit#7D6290 · floor_color
glass vein#7DFFCA · glass_color
thrust#DD5AFF · thrust_color
wall#3A264E · wall_color
hull shadow#2F1E3C · derived
  1. Asymmetric taper. One flank is always longer or sharper; the whole form pulls toward a single dominant point. Never mirror-symmetric, never a blunt box.
  2. Off-centre window clusters. A handful of ports grouped along the tallest ridge or spine, tracing a vein of light through the material — never a uniform grid.
  3. Odd-count fixtures. Thrusters, pods, berth arms in threes and fives, unevenly spaced, rather than mirrored pairs.
  4. Veins mark stress, not holes. Bioluminescent glass runs along load paths and edges. It is structure made visible, not a row of punched windows.
  5. Rounded, flowing volumes. Rims caught in light. Interiors are many-sided "circle" rooms joined by overlap; edge veins trace every room.

01·A — Ships

Thruster ports. Every mount in thrusters now carries a drawn port — for the Concord, a grown asymmetric resin flare lined with a glass-vein hairline, in odd-count clusters (directive 3). The magenta flame is a separate shape tagged class="flame": it is not extracted into parts — Ship._draw_thrusters still draws it live — but it is drawn narrower than the port mouth and rooted inside it, so in game the jet reads as firing out of the nozzle.

Chapter 02 — The Drossholt Company

Bolted together from whatever a supply run can carry

A frontier trading and salvage outfit running the outposts at the edge of known space. Where the Vherathi grow their architecture, Drossholt bolts it together out of whatever plate and scavenged hull segments a supply run can carry — blunt, riveted, and built to be patched rather than beautiful. — cultures.json · "drossholt"
hull / wall#C89664 · metal_color
plate lit#DAB488 · floor_color
window / lamp#FFC94F · glass_color
thrust#FF8C3C · thrust_color
rivet / trim#5A4130 · wall_trim_color
patch plate#8A6845 · derived
  1. Blunt rectangular volumes. Boxes bolted together along visible seams. No tapering, no asymmetry for its own sake — the functional opposite of a grown hull.
  2. Even rows along seams. Windows and warning lights run in regular, evenly-spaced lines following structural seams, never clustered.
  3. Mirrored pairs. Thrusters, lamps, fixtures come in matched, evenly-spaced pairs — odd counts look wrong here.
  4. Seams are the ornament. Rivet lines, weld beads and mismatched patch-plate carry all the visual interest. One panel is always a slightly different shade.
  5. Right angles, built to be patched. Rectangular rooms, corridors meeting at 90°; every join is a place you could bolt on more later.

02·A — Ships

Thruster ports. Each mount gets a bolted rectangular nozzle housing in patch-metal with a riveted edge — mirrored pairs, dead even (directives 2·3). The orange flame is tagged class="flame" and stays procedural (Ship._draw_thrusters); in the plate it is drawn narrower than the housing and beneath it, so the exhaust appears to fire from the throat.

Chapter 03 — Interiors

Rooms that read as one place

An interior is a set of room polygons — rectangles, concave shapes, or many-sided "circle" N-gons — whose union is the walkable area, so two rooms that overlap read as one connected space. The culture sets the floor / wall / trim colour and drives an interior_decoration generator that traces every room edge. Structures are furniture placed at a point (they carry a collision footprint); decorations are cosmetic floor / wall decals with none. — location_screen.py · normalize_room / _build_culture_decorations

Shipped. Both station interiors and both moon cities were rebuilt to these plans (commit a243e96); each plate below is the worked example that model follows.

  1. Vherathi — flowing & grown. Many-sided circle rooms and wide organic corridors, joined by overlap, never a grid. A big central concourse; smaller pods bud off it. Doorways are just where two rooms cross.
  2. Rims in light. edge_veins insets a bioluminescent-glass thread just inside every room's edge — structure made visible, tracing the whole perimeter.
  1. Drossholt — rectangular & bolted. Plain rectangular rooms and straight corridors meeting at 90°. A linear plan you could bolt another bay onto: airlock → transfer → bay.
  2. Seams studded. seam_rivets studs every room edge with evenly-spaced perpendicular tick marks — the rivet line is the ornament.

03·C — Room-edge accents

edge accents · detail

edge_veins vs. seam_rivets

Both are generators — the interior authors nothing, the culture's interior_decoration block runs against every room polygon at load. edge_veins pulls each vertex ~8 units toward the centroid and strokes the closed inset ring in glass_color. seam_rivets walks each edge and drops a short perpendicular tick every spacing units.

vherathi
edge_veins · inset 10 · width 2 · #7DFFCA
drossholt
seam_rivets · spacing 44 · length 12 · width 3 · #E1C396
cultures.json → <culture>.interior_decoration. A new culture gets its own generator or reuses one with different params.

03·D — Placing the furniture

decoration placement · concourse

Placement rules

  • Desk faces the entry. The concierge / duty desk sits just inside the room the portal opens into, turned toward whoever walks in — the first NPC you meet.
  • Benches hug the wall. Backs to the edge, following the arc (Vherathi) or flush to a straight wall (Drossholt) — never mid-floor.
  • Lights on a rhythm. Columns / work-lights at even intervals round the rim, plus one flanking each side of the desk. Drossholt adds them along the corridor line.
  • Focal props, offset. Fern basins / scrub tubs and lounge pods sit off centre and off the through-lines, so they decorate without blocking.
  • Keep the lanes clear. A furniture-free path from the portal to the desk and on to every doorway. Nothing with a footprint lands on it.
  • Cluster the working stuff. Drossholt crates / drums stack against the bay's back wall near the work area, not spread evenly.
structures
[{x, y, building_type}] · each has a footprint
decorations
[{layer, shape, …, color}] · cosmetic, no footprint
check
no structure footprint may overlap a portal or a doorway-crossing
Per-interior in the system JSON. Furniture types come from building_types.json (see 01·D / 02·D); the placement — not the catalogue — is what these rules govern.

What shipped

All of it is now in config/stories/default (commits 870254e, 10f392d, a243e96, 0f9f30e, 1df0e18) and story.json is at 1.10.0 — a save from before warns on load. The catalogue below is where each piece lives.

Ship stats

config/stories/default/ship_types.json — name, thrust, velocity, rotation, size, cost, cargo, slots. New keys prefixed vherathi_ / drossholt_ like the two that exist.

Ship geometry

graphics.json → ships — culture, thrusters, and the extracted parts / local_points / outline_color. parts is the whole drawn hull; local_points is collision only. Base colours pull from the culture, never hardcoded.

Outfits (worn)

graphics.json → outfits — the *_color fields plus extras (shoulder, sash, visor, chest_plate, backpack, belt, badge). Roles are set on NPCs.

Ship equipment

ship_outfits.json — the buyable modules (laser, afterburner, cargo, hull). Separate from worn outfits; referenced by slot type.

Buildings & furniture

building_types.json — shape, points/radius, windows, window_shape, footprint (collision). Furniture is just a building with a small footprint.

Stations

graphics.json → space_stations — shape, local_points, size, rotation_speed, landing_distance, plus parts + outline_color from the plate. LandingSite._draw_station now draws the parts silhouette directly (the flat hull + window dots are skipped when parts is present).

Interiors

Per-interior in the system JSON (sol_alpha.json / keplers_reach.json) → station.interiors.default and moon.interiors.city: rooms, portals, structures, decorations. Edge accents come free from the culture's interior_decoration.

Interior decals

Per-interior decorations in the system JSON + the per-culture interior_decoration generator (edge_veins / seam_rivets).
