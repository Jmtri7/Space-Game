Default story — the shared body & the culture-neutral kit

One body, issued a hundred ways

Every character in the game — the player on foot, every station NPC, every ship's pilot — is drawn from a single Person body: a foot oval, a tapering torso, a head. An outfit only recolours it and bolts on flat accessory pieces. This atlas gave that body legs (shipped), and re-draws the graphics.json outfits that aren't tied to a culture — the standard suit, the station crew, the command tier — on the new model.

The legged body and the hardware shipped. Chapter 01's redesign is in person.py (legs, boots, hip line, shared walk cycle), with leg_color as an optional outfit key. Chapter 05's Standard Issue ships, station and buildings are in config/stories/default — silhouettes extracted from these plates into parts — and the Procyon Gate system makes the standard_issue culture live in game. Only the culture-neutral outfit redraws (Chapters 02–04) are still mockups: the Person renderer is colour-key driven, not per-outfit geometry, so those wait on a parts-style figure renderer. The ship plates (05·A) also carry explicit thruster ports, extracted into parts; the Standard Ring's ring modules and the Issue Tender's docking collar extract as transparent-hole rings, and the pale-blue exhaust stays procedural, drawn from the nozzle mouth.

Chapter 01 — The shared body

Give the Person legs

The body was deliberately minimal: three shapes, shaded from one skin tone, never changed by an outfit. But a single foot-oval read more like a chess pawn than a person once you stood next to it. This shipped — the shared Person now stands on two legs and two boots and walks with them, so the change landed once and every character got it.

Current vs. proposed SHIPPED

person.py · Person.draw()

The torso and head are unchanged. Chapter 05 — Standard Issue hardware

Not grown, not scavenged — issued

The command tier implies a third party: a civil authority that runs the neutral stations, licenses the ships, and stencils a registration number on everything. Where the Vherathi grow their hardware and the Drossholt bolt theirs, this faction issues it — factory-painted, colour-coded, built from interchangeable modules to one spec. This chapter gives that look ships, a station, buildings, props, and an interior, so "Standard Issue" is a design language and not just a rack of grey suits.

  1. Prefab modules. A hull is a spine of identical bays; a station is identical rings on a spine; a building is identical floors stacked. Interchangeable, replaceable, numbered.
  2. One corner radius. Rounded rectangles everywhere — machined and finished. Not the Vherathi organic curve, not the Drossholt raw right angle; a consistent small fillet on every corner.
  3. Painted livery. Body colour + a contrast stripe along the centreline + amber hazard chevrons at every thruster, hatch and edge. A stencilled registration (SF-###) on the hull.
  4. Clean symmetry. Mirrored and evenly spaced like Drossholt, but flush — no visible patch plates, no mismatched shades, windows in a regular grid.
  5. Interiors: spine and bays. A straight central concourse with identical rooms budding off both sides at regular intervals; every doorway marked with a painted safety line and hazard chevrons.

05·A — Ships

Thruster ports. The regulation nozzle — a rounded-corner housing in si_panel grey with a dark outline and an amber hazard chevron painted over the throat — is the reference the other cultures adapt for their own thrusters mounts. The pale-blue flame (thrust_color) is tagged class="flame", stays procedural (Ship._draw_thrusters), and is drawn narrower than the nozzle and beneath it — so it is not pulled into parts, only the port is.

What landed, and what's left

The body shape — done

game/world/person.py: the foot-oval draw is now two legs + two boots, BODY_HEIGHT 13→11, new leg/boot constants, and the outline pass extended. LocationScreen.LABEL_HEIGHT_ABOVE 38→42.

The walk cycle — done

step_toward → _advance_walk advances walk_phase by distance walked and ramps walk_intensity; draw() eases it out on idle frames. 5 tests in TestPersonWalkCycle.

leg_color key — done

New optional outfit key, one .get("leg_color", _shade(suit,-22)) in draw() — same pattern as every other accessory key. No existing outfit sets it yet.

Outfit colour adds — shipped

graphics.json → outfits: the suggested one-key adds all landed — dockworker chest_plate (hi-vis band), medic badge (cross), mechanic belt. The full outfit redraws stay mockups (they need a parts-style figure renderer first).

Standard Issue hardware — shipped

The standard_issue culture (Sol Federation) is in cultures.json; issue_shuttle/lighter/cutter/tender, station_ring, and issue_block/shed/bollard/bench are in the usual files, each with parts extracted from its plate here. The Procyon Gate system (Procyon Ring station + Garrison Row moon, story 1.10.0) places all of it in a live system — the culture-neutral kit and the Sol Federation now exist in game.

It was a shape change, not a config edit. The single Person body is shared by the player, every station and moon NPC, and every AI ship's pilot — so this one change redrew everyone at once, in every interior. No save impact: outfits and NPCs are rebuilt fresh from story config on every load, never stored — so an old save just loads its people on the new legs.

Companion atlas: Resin & Rivets covers the two cultures' ships, stations, buildings and their culture-specific outfits (Vherathi hardsuits, Drossholt vacsuits, honour guards). Same body model — so the legs land there too.
