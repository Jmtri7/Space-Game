The Grounded Person model

One proportion pass on the shared body in person_figure.py — a short torso over long legs (hip up at ~47% of height), a trapezius yoke that sweeps up behind the neck, rounded shoulders, a masc / femme split on the torso and hip width, and a flat one-direction shade down one side of each limb — then the face kit worked out on it.

The model has since been redrawn for looks. The head is smaller and sits higher, on a slimmer neck that is actually visible instead of resting its chin on the collarbone; the skull is widest at the cheekbone and tapers through a narrower jaw to a small rounded chin. The face kit that goes on it is new work: almond eyes with a real upper lid — iris, pupil and a catchlight under a lash line that crops the top of the iris — a fine tapered brow that thickens over the inner third, a nose built from a slim bridge shadow into a small soft tip instead of a dark blob, and a two-part mouth, an upper lip with a cupid's bow over a fuller lower one, its seam riding a touch higher at the corners. The head carries the same one-direction shade the limbs do, shaped as a leaf across the far cheek and jaw. On the body: the leg gets a thigh / knee / calf line, the boot a proper foot, the hand a tapered mitt instead of a ball, and the arms hang closer at rest. Then the hairstyles and skin tones, which way it faces, and how each holds up as the figure shrinks on screen.

1  The body

Head-count ticks up the left — a little under seven now that the head has come down to 5.2 u, with the short torso over long legs. The head sits high enough to leave about a unit of bare neck above the collar, which is most of what makes the figure read upright rather than hunched. The top of the torso curves up behind the neck as a trapezius yoke, then sweeps down and out over the shoulder cap to meet the domed arm top in one smooth line. The waist pinches, then the hip curves back out — a convex flare, not a straight taper. The side shade is a strip that follows that curve. Below the hip the leg has three stops — thigh, knee, calf — so it carries a line instead of tapering straight to the ankle, and the boot is a foot: a low toe box over a compact heel.

Grounded · femme

Keeps the chunky, sprite-legible silhouette — nothing downstream (walk cycle, outfit colour keys, accessory slots) needs to move, only LEG_HEIGHT / ARM_LENGTH to match the new limb lengths. The walk cycle keeps its shape too — arms swinging opposite each other, counter to the stride.

The two builds. There's no neutral — every figure on this page is one or the other. They differ only in the torso silhouette and the hip / arm-attach / leg-stance widths: masc widens the upper torso and narrows the hip; femme narrows the shoulders, cuts the waist and widens the hip. Same head, limbs, height, and walk.

2  Walk cycle

The settled gait, live. swing = sin(walk_phase) drives the legs (±16° about the hip, forward foot lifting); the arms swing opposite each other and counter to the stride — the natural human walk — with a small body dip at the extremes.

This changes the current in-game _arm_swing, which sways both arms the same way. Everything else in Person._leg_stance / _arm_swing stays — same phase variable, same amplitudes, same body bob.

Reduced-motion: the animation starts paused.

3  Facing

The figure reads three-quarter — the feet point the way it faces and the head is turned that way too (features clustered to that side, near ear showing). As built it faces left; face: -1 mirrors the whole group to face right — in game this is Person.facing.

Left is the model as built. Right is the same figure with translate(132,0) scale(-1,1) on the group. Shown mid-stride so the swinging limbs and the foot direction read.

4  Hair & skin

Hairstyles on the Grounded head — the same egg-shaped head, ears and features the full figure draws, at a different scale — each with a different skin token. Flat single-colour fills, and none of them is a free-hand outline: every style is built from the skull from a handful of numbers, so it cannot float off the crown, balloon past the temples or stop in a blunt horizontal chop. The outer edge is the skull's own profile — traced through its real vertices — pushed out by a lift that peaks over the crown (0.02 face-radii for the buzz, 0.22 for the curls) and fades to nothing at the temples, so the hair melts into the head silhouette. Each crescent ends at a temple point kept above the brow, leaving the temple bare and the ear clear, and its inner hairline sweeps from a high crown peak down to meet that same point — so every style tapers to a sideburn. Distinct shapes: a snug buzz, a rounded crop, a left side part with a stepped parting, a receding pair of temple bays around a forelock, a scalloped ring of curl clumps, a slicked crown for the bun (knot tucked into it) and the high ponytail, and a crown plus a panel down each side for the long — that panel starts on the crescent's own temple point and hands back to the hairline short of it, so the two shapes meet without a wedge of bare temple between them or a spur of hair standing off the side of the head. Kade is the flush buzz-cut shadow — one fill mixed from skin and hair, built the same way with zero lift.

Face kit — the egg-shaped head, almond eyes (sclera, iris, pupil, catchlight and a lash line that crops the iris, each rotated ~6° so the outer corner sits higher), the fine tapered brow, the bridge-and-tip nose, the two-part lips and the low D-ears become part of the base head; one new optional key set (hair enum + hair_color) resolved in Person._fig_color / draw, with a fig.HAIR table baked the same way as ACC — the stubble option is a single fill a shade between skin and hair, clipped to the crown, so a shaved head still reads; defaults reproduce the current look, and NPC rosters / graphics.json opt in per character. (This page draws the parts with no outline at all — fills only. Each arm, leg and the torso carries a fixed shade polygon down its screen-right side, the head a leaf-shaped one across its far cheek and jaw, and the neck a short band where the head's jaw casts onto it — each shape hand-built off the part's own anchor points, riding the same walk transform, filled at shade(fill, -24). The torso shade is a strip whose inner and outer edges both parallel the body's side curve, so it stays off to one side; the neck shade's lower edge parallels the chin and its deepest point sits toward the facing side. Hair that hangs behind the back (long, ponytail) draws before the body so the torso covers its inner half. The head is drawn turned toward the facing direction — skull off-centre by HEAD_DX, features over by FACE_DX and scaled FACE_S, the hair riding the skull centre (it is built off the skull profile, so it cannot have an offset of its own), near ear showing, far ear covered — and face: -1 just mirrors the whole group so a right-facing figure turns its head right. Hairstyles are flat single-colour fills, and HAIR_SHAPE is generated, not typed: buildHair() walks the skull profile (headR(), sampled on HEAD_SHAPE's own vertices so a flat style never cuts a chord inside the head) and offsets it by a per-style crown / temple lift, stops at tempAng, and closes the shape with a hairline curve (peak, q, plus optional dip for a widow's peak, bay for temple recession and skew / sweep / part / jog for the side part). curls adds a rim of lump circles riding that same edge, and long hangs a panel down each side of the face. In the baked table these become plain point lists, exactly as now. In person_figure.py the outline is its own slightly-larger shape behind each part, per the atlas pipeline; dropping it means removing that backing shape, and each shade bakes as one extra fill in the part's figure_parts entry.)
