The Grounded Person model

One proportion pass on the shared body in person_figure.py — a short torso over long legs (hip up at ~47% of height), a trapezius yoke that sweeps up behind the neck, rounded shoulders, a masc / femme split on the torso and hip width, and a flat one-direction shade down one side of each limb — then the face kit worked out on it.

The model has since been redrawn for looks. The head is smaller and sits higher, on a slimmer neck that is actually visible instead of resting its chin on the collarbone; the skull is widest at the cheekbone and tapers through a narrower jaw to a small rounded chin. The face kit that goes on it is new work: almond eyes with a real upper lid — iris, pupil and a catchlight under a lash line that crops the top of the iris — a fine tapered brow that thickens over the inner third, a nose built from a slim bridge shadow into a small soft tip instead of a dark blob, and a two-part mouth, an upper lip with a cupid's bow over a fuller lower one, its seam riding a touch higher at the corners. The head carries the same one-direction shade the limbs do, shaped as a leaf across the far cheek and jaw. On the body: the leg gets a thigh / knee / calf line, the boot a proper foot, the hand a tapered mitt instead of a ball, and the arms hang closer at rest. The hair was redrawn on the same terms: still generated from the skull, but in three tones instead of one — a shade ribbon down the far side and a sheen over the near crown, both following the hair's own contour and tapering to nothing at each end, so nothing reads as a second colour laid on across a straight edge — with volume on a plateau lift rather than a crown bulge (a bulge only makes the skull taller, which is what helmet hair is) and a ripple over that, the hairline cut into pointed locks that straddle it, a drawn parting on the side part, a rippled silhouette on the curls, a knot that sits proud of the crown for the bun, and a long style whose side locks leave the crown along its own curve over a drape that falls past the shoulders in shadow. Then the skin tones, which way it faces, and how each holds up as the figure shrinks on screen.

1  The body

Head-count ticks up the left — a little under seven now that the head has come down to 5.2 u, with the short torso over long legs. The head sits high enough to leave about a unit of bare neck above the collar, which is most of what makes the figure read upright rather than hunched. The top of the torso curves up behind the neck as a trapezius yoke, then sweeps down and out over the shoulder cap to meet the domed arm top in one smooth line. The waist pinches, then the hip curves back out — a convex flare, not a straight taper. The side shade is a strip that follows that curve. Below the hip the leg has three stops — thigh, knee, calf — so it carries a line instead of tapering straight to the ankle, and the boot is a foot: a low toe box over a compact heel.

Grounded · femme

Keeps the chunky, sprite-legible silhouette — nothing downstream (walk cycle, outfit colour keys, accessory slots) needs to move, only LEG_HEIGHT / ARM_LENGTH to match the new limb lengths. The walk cycle keeps its shape too — arms swinging opposite each other, counter to the stride.

The two builds. There's no neutral — every figure on this page is one or the other. They differ only in the torso silhouette and the hip / arm-attach / leg-stance widths: masc widens the upper torso and narrows the hip; femme narrows the shoulders, cuts the waist and widens the hip. Same head, limbs, height, and walk.

2  Walk cycle

The settled gait, live. swing = sin(walk_phase) drives the legs (±16° about the hip, forward foot lifting); the arms swing opposite each other and counter to the stride — the natural human walk — with a small body dip at the extremes.

This changes the current in-game _arm_swing, which sways both arms the same way. Everything else in Person._leg_stance / _arm_swing stays — same phase variable, same amplitudes, same body bob.

Reduced-motion: the animation starts paused.

4  Hair & skin

Hairstyles on the Grounded head — the same egg-shaped head, ears and features the full figure draws, at a different scale — each with a different skin token. None of them is a free-hand outline: every style is built from the skull from a handful of numbers, so it cannot float off the crown, balloon past the temples or stop in a blunt horizontal chop. The outer edge is the skull's own profile — traced through its real vertices — pushed out by a lift that is a plateau, not a bulge: it ramps up over the first sixth of the sweep, holds right across the sides and the crown (0.08 face-radii for the buzz, 0.43 for the curls), and ramps back down into the far sideburn. That is the difference between hair and a helmet. A lift that only swells over the crown leaves the outline an offset copy of the skull, however deep you make it; a plateau stands the mass off the head everywhere, and because the ramp leaves the skull tangentially rather than as a cusp, the two ends don't spike. On top of it every style carries a ripple, gentle on most and pulled into clumps on the curls, so no edge is a clean arc. Each crescent ends at a temple point kept above the brow, leaving the temple bare and the ear clear, and its inner hairline sweeps from a high crown peak down to meet that same point — so every style tapers to a sideburn.

Three tones, and no straight edges anywhere. A style bakes into a part list rather than a single shape, and every piece on it past the fill is the same object: a contour ribbon that runs along the hair's own outer edge between two angles, swells in the middle and tapers to a point at both ends. The shade is one down the far side, the sheen a thinner one held just off the edge over the near side of the crown, the parting another. None of them has an end you can see or a straight chord across the mass — which is exactly what a shade laid on as a flat wedge looks like — and none can be deeper than the hair beneath it, because each ray's depth is capped by how far it can travel in from the edge before it reaches the hairline. The tones are scaled off the hair colour rather than offset from it, so near-black hair still separates instead of clamping to the same black. The figure and the head card walk the same list.

And the hairline is cut into locks. A smooth swept hairline is what made every flat style read as a swim cap; the fuller ones now hang it in pointed locks that straddle the curve — each point below it, each notch above — at uneven depths, so a fringe reads as cut hair rather than a row of bumps sitting on a line. Distinct shapes: a snug buzz, a cropped fringe over a crown that sits a little off centre, a left side part — combed rather than cut, so no locks: a stepped parting, the parting itself opened up over the crown, and a hairline that sweeps low across one brow and back off the other, a receding pair of temple bays around a forelock, a curl mass whose silhouette itself is rippled into clumps (no circles pasted on top of a smooth one), a slicked crown under a knot that sits proud of it for the bun, a tapered rope of a tail for the ponytail, and for the long, a lock down each side of the face over a drape behind.

Where the pieces meet is where a hairstyle goes wrong, and each junction is now built rather than eyeballed. The long lock leaves the crescent along the crescent's own curve instead of stepping straight out to its full width, which used to put a horn on each side of the head; its inner edge hugs the skull while there is a cheek to hug and then holds a line past the jaw, so it is thin where it presses to the cheekbone and full where it hangs free, and never floats off the face with daylight behind it; and it hands back to the hairline short of the temple, since running the hairline all the way out doubles the loop back on itself. The back drape starts inside the front hair's silhouette rather than poking a corner out over each temple, and it draws in the shade tone — it is behind the head, and at the same tone as the locks in front of it the whole side of the head merges into one flat slab. Kade is the flush buzz-cut shadow — one fill mixed from skin and hair, built the same way with zero lift, and the one style with no shade or sheen: it is a shadow on the scalp, not a mass of hair.

Each hair colour resolves to three tones through hairTone(), scaled rather than offset so black hair still separates. In the baked table these become plain point lists per tone, exactly as now.
