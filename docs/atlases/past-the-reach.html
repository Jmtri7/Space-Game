Seven proposed cultures · distinct silhouettes, distinct kit

Past the Reach

The default story has three cultures and they read as three — grown, bolted, issued. This atlas proposes seven more for the edge of the map, and holds each to a harder standard: an exotic ship silhouette you'd know in one frame, and an outfit with a signature piece no other culture wears — an ember-slit mask, an antler headdress, a helm of eye-bubbles.

None of this is in the game. Every plate is a MOCKUP. Ships are strokeless <polygon>/<circle> and could be extracted into parts; the outfit redraws wait on a parts-style figure renderer, the same as the Standard Issue outfit chapters. Three of these cultures are brand new (Kaethar, Vetl, Salt Crows); the other four refine the earlier frontier sketches with far more detail. The shared body here carries the cinched waist now in gen_si.figure_parts / person_figure.py — belts sit at the waist, roughly halfway up, and every torso is an hourglass whether or not an outfit covers it.

Before the chapters — the principle

Every culture its own kit

The shared Person body is colour-key driven: an outfit recolours it and toggles a fixed set of accessory pieces — a collar, a sash, a backpack, a visor. It's efficient, but it means a Vherathi hardsuit and a Drossholt vacsuit are the same silhouette in different colours. The fix is a small set of culture-signature shapes — new geometry, one or two per culture, that nobody else uses. This section shows the idea applied back to the three shipped cultures; the seven chapters below each carry their own.

Chapter 01 — Deeprock Mining Consortium

Built around the job

Already a faction in pilots.json (Oskar Lindqvist, mining_foreman) with no look yet. Hardware built around the job it does, painted only where a warning is needed.

hull / wall#5C524A
hull shadow#413A34
glass / lights#FFE078
thrust#FF9646
trim#D6B03C

cultures.json → deeprock — proposed, not in config

  1. Front-heavy, functional. One dominant forward volume - a crusher jaw or ore scoop - a fat segmented tank body behind it, ancillary boxes (cabin, chute) clamped on wherever they cleared the load. Never tidy.
  2. Almost no windows. Light is a few harsh floodlamps clustered at the working end, drawn from glass_color. The crew works by them, not by a view.
  3. Hazard, not livery. Trim-yellow striping and chevrons (wall_trim_color) edge every intake, hatch and thruster. Everything else stays bare, seamed, riveted metal_color.
  4. Industrial plant that flies. Segment seams and rivet rows on every barrel, a conveyor ridge down the spine, blunt quad-thruster housings. It reads as machinery first.

Chapter 02 — The Ashfall Rite

The ember in the stone

A close order that keeps to the burnt worlds. Where the Vherathi grow their hulls and the Drossholt bolt theirs, the Kessari fire theirs - dark ceramic carved like a reliquary, lit only where the material is still cooling.

hull / wall#22201E
hull shadow#33302C
glass / lights#FF823C
thrust#DC4628
trim#8A7A5C

cultures.json → kessari — proposed, not in config

  1. One carved mass. A tall base-heavy monolith with stepped flying-buttress flanks, recessed relief grooves across the face, and a finial spire cluster at the nose. Roughly symmetric, one edge always left hand-irregular.
  2. The ember seam. A bright ladder of glass_color down the spine with cross-ribs, a radial rose-window aperture cluster near the nose, a matching glow through the exhaust. Everything else is near-black metal_color.
  3. Ceremonial fittings. The exhaust is an ornate perforated censer housing, not a nozzle; buttress edges carry an ash-grey trim line.
  4. Cell, not cabin. Interiors stay narrow, dim, high-contrast: black floor and walls, one bright line of light down the centre of every room.

Chapter 03 — The Meridian Free Ports

Seen arriving

A loose confederation of independent trading ports, rich and fond of showing it. Brass and cream, layered decks, swept sail-fins and lantern light - built to look good coming down the ramp.

hull shadow#6A5030

  1. A banded lantern hull. One long tapering hull divided into decks by brass bands, not stacked balls. Symmetric and centred - not the Vherathi organic asymmetry.
  2. Ornament is the point. Brass filigree scrollwork framing the fore hull, a stylised sunburst figurehead prow, a crowning lantern finial - every edge is trimmed (wall_trim_color).
  3. Windows and lanterns on show. An arched row of warm windows per deck plus a lantern pair at each end - a Meridian captain wants to be seen.
  4. Swept sail-fins. Two large decorative fins sweep well aft from mid-hull, scalloped along the trailing edge, brass-ribbed.

Chapter 04 — The Theln Drift

Never makes port

Nomads who never stay anywhere long. Ships are tensioned membrane on a light frame - translucent, kite-like, asymmetric - strung with running lights like rigging.

Chapter 05 — The Kaethar Directorate

Correct, and armed

NEW CULTURE A cold, hierarchical military power that garrisons the lanes and expects its transponder logs read back to it. Hard angles, gunmetal, one warning colour and nothing else.

hull / wall#3A3F47
hull shadow#282C33
glass / lights#CFD6DE
thrust#9FC0D8
trim#D6402C

cultures.json → kaethar — proposed, not in config

  1. Arrowhead and rail. A sharp narrow nose widening to a blocky midbody, a raised spinal rail running its full length to a muzzle at the tip. Every corner is a hard angle - no fillet, no curve.
  2. Forward-swept everything. Wing pylons and turret nacelles sweep forward, toward the target, not back. Blocky hardpoint pods on the wings.
  3. One warning colour. Sharp red chevrons (wall_trim_color) along every wing leading edge, a hard geometric unit sigil on the nose, red rank/sensor bars. The hull itself stays flat gunmetal, recessed panel seams and nothing more.
  4. Interiors: rank and file. A straight spine, identical cells, a painted line you walk between - the Standard Issue plan with the warmth removed.

Chapter 06 — The Vetl

Grown, not built

NEW CULTURE A shamanistic people whose ships read as sea-creatures - broad, boneless, whip-tailed - and whose crews wear antler, hide and bead. Spirit-teal light where another culture would put a running lamp.

  1. A creature silhouette. A broad flat manta body - a smooth wide lens, widest at mid, tapering both ways - with forward cephalic horn-prongs and a long tapering whip tail ending in a barb.
  2. Bone and hide. A fan of pale wall_trim_color rib-veins from the spine to the wing edge, a dorsal ridge of small spines, mottled darker hide patches for texture. No panel lines - it isn't panelled.

Chapter 07 — The Salt Crows

Cut from three other ships

NEW CULTURE Scavengers and raiders. Nothing they fly or wear was built as a whole - it's rust, tar, and scavenged brass, with a ram on the front and a crow daubed on the side.

  1. Asymmetric and kinked. A bent spine, wider to one side, a heavy pointed ram prow with reinforcement plates. Deliberately lopsided.

Wiring in

cultures.json

Seven new entries — name, description, the colour keys shown, an interior_decoration generator, a theme string. Self-contained; assets reference them by key.

ship_types.json + graphics.json

One ship type + graphics entry per culture. Each ship silhouette can be extracted from its plate into parts via extract_atlas.py / apply_parts.py — add a row to the T table. The Vetl's soft tail glow and the Theln's translucent membrane have no engine support yet (Ship.draw draws flat opaque polygons) — they'd ship as solid tinted shapes until a translucency / additive layer exists.

graphics.json → outfits & the figure renderer

The recoloured base of each outfit is buildable now (existing *_color keys). The signature pieces are new geometry — an ember-slit mask, antlers, eye-bubble clusters — so they need a parts-style figure renderer (the gen_si.figure_parts generator already produces exactly this kind of data; build_person_figure.py bakes it into person_figure.py). Adding a figure_signature table keyed by culture would land all ten redesigns, shipped cultures included.

routines & pilots

Kaethar wants a patrol/picket routine, the Salt Crows a raider, Deeprock a mining loop, the Vetl an explorer. A culture is half a faction without a routine and a pilot roster — see the routine proposals discussed alongside this atlas.

Adding cultures is additive; adding ship types and outfits is not free. A new ship type shows up in every shipyard list and a new outfit in every outfitter — an old save loading into a shop sees the new stock. That's a story-version bump (see SAVE_SYSTEM.md), not a breakage: NPCs and outfits are rebuilt from story config on load, never stored.

Companion atlases: Resin & Rivets (Vherathi + Drossholt) and Standard Issue (the shared body + Sol Federation) — the shipped record. Same visual system, same strokeless rule, same figure_parts body.
